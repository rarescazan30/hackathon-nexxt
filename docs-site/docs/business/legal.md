# Legal context

:::caution Not legal advice
A research summary for the pitch. A lawyer should confirm before building it for real.
:::

## Short answer

**No new law is needed.** The model works if a **licensed partner holds the money**, not LifeGrowth. That takes partnerships and contracts (months), not legislation (years).

## Why LifeGrowth can't hold the money itself

- Taking money from people to keep it is **deposit-taking**: only banks (licensed by BNR) may do it.
- A **payment institution** (PSD2) may hold client money only to execute payments, safeguarded and separate (PSD2 art. 10 and 18), not as savings.

## Realistic paths

| Option | How | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Partner bank** (recommended) | The money sits in an account in the user's name at a partner bank; a revenue-share contract gives LifeGrowth part of the interest. | months |
| **B · Banking-as-a-Service** | LifeGrowth as an agent of a licensed e-money / payment institution. Holding money on purpose for a month is a grey zone. | months, with legal sign-off |
| **C · Own licence** | E-money or payment institution licence from BNR. | a year or more |

## The Pillar III side

Promoting Pillar III must go through an **authorised marketing agent** of a pension administrator (Law 204/2006, ASF Norm 16/2013). Easiest path: a partnership with one administrator. Romanian platforms already do this, so the path is proven.

## Bank data (PSD2)

Reading transactions needs an **AISP** licence (BNR, Law 209/2019), or a **licensed aggregator** that already has it. Paying from the bank account would need **PIS**; LifeGrowth uses card payments through Stripe instead.

## Must-haves

- Clear terms: the one-month hold and who keeps the interest (done in the prototype).
- The user can see where the money is and get it back if they cancel during the month.
- Personal data in statements: data-processing agreements, minimal data sent to AI.
